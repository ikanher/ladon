35: \begin{abstract}
36: We give a conventional proof for an ideal adaptive training mechanism in
37: which each user independently receives a hidden, uniform $k$-subset of the
38: $T$ release positions.  The result has three parts: an executable
39: exact-rational add/remove calibration, an attained least scale for privacy
40: uniformly over all admissible adaptive oracles under add/remove adjacency, and
41: an attained least scale for direct replace-one privacy.  The finite
42: calibrator is add/remove only and is minimal only within its checked finite
43: candidate family.  The two exact thresholds are noncomputable real numbers.
44: Here ``deterministic'' describes the proof and calibration, not the randomized
45: hidden schedules; neither Monte Carlo estimation nor EVR is used.
46: The proof isolates the hidden-schedule mixture, the adaptive Gaussian channel,
47: the signed interpolation required for direct replacement, the lower
48: realizations, and the topology behind threshold attainment.
49: \end{abstract}

51: \section{Main theorem}
52: 
53: \paragraph{Setup and notation.}
54: Let $\mathcal X$ be a record type with decidable equality, so a dataset is a
55: finite set $D\subseteq\mathcal X$.  Fix $T>0$, $0\leq k\leq T$, an output
56: dimension $d$, and a measurable adaptive oracle.  From the previously released
57: history $h_{<t}$ and a record $u$, the oracle returns
58: $q_t(h_{<t},u)\in\R^d$.  Each user independently receives
59: \[
60:   S_u\sim\operatorname{Unif}
61:   \{S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,T-1\}:|S|=k\}.
62: \]
63: Starting from the \emph{zero initial history}, the ideal mechanism releases
64: \begin{equation}\label{eq:release}
65:   Y_t=\sum_{u\in D}\mathbf1_{\{t\in S_u\}}
66:        \clip\!\left(q_t(Y_{<t},u)\right)+\sigma Z_t,
67:   \qquad 0\leq t<T,
68: \end{equation}
69: where $\|\clip(v)\|_2\leq1$ and the $Z_t$ are independent standard
70: $d$-dimensional Gaussians.  Schedules are hidden and marginalized only after
71: the complete conditional transcript law is formed.
72: 
73: Add/remove adjacency inserts or removes one record.  Direct replace-one
74: adjacency compares
75: \[
76:  D_L=C\cup\{u\},\qquad D_R=C\cup\{v\},
77:  \qquad u\neq v,\quad u,v\notin C.
78: \]
79: For probability measures $P,Q$, write
80: \[
81:  H_\varepsilon(P,Q)
82:  =\sup_{0\leq f\leq1}
83:    \left(\int f\,dP-e^\varepsilon\int f\,dQ\right).
84: \]
85: Approximate differential privacy means that this quantity is at most
86: $\delta$ in both directions for every adjacent pair.
87: 
88: \begin{theorem}[Deterministic $k$-out-of-$T$ accountant]\label{thm:main}
89: Let $T,k\in\N$ and $\varepsilon,\delta\in\Q$ satisfy
90: \[
91:  0<T,\qquad k\leq T,\qquad 0\leq\varepsilon,\qquad 0<\delta.
92: \]
93: The following hold.
94: \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
95: \item A terminating exact-rational procedure returns
96:   $\widehat\sigma\in\Q_{>0}$.  For every record type with decidable equality,
97:   every $d$, and every measurable adaptive oracle, \eqref{eq:release} at
98:   $\widehat\sigma$ is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-private for add/remove adjacency.
99:   The returned scale is the minimum among an independently checked total
100:   fallback and the accepted members of a finite public rational grid generated
101:   from the request.
102:   This is an add/remove theorem and a grid-relative minimum, not a direct
103:   replace-one calibration or a global minimum over $\R_{>0}$.
104: 
105: \item If additionally $0<k$ and $\delta<1$, there is a noncomputable real
106:   $\sigma_{\mathrm{AR}}^*>0$ such that, for every $\sigma>0$,
107:   \[
108:     \sigma_{\mathrm{AR}}^*\leq\sigma
109:     \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
110:     \eqref{eq:release}\text{ is add/remove private for every such }
111:     \mathcal X,d,\text{ and oracle}.
112:   \]
113:   Privacy holds at $\sigma_{\mathrm{AR}}^*$.
114: 
115: \item Under the same additional assumptions there is a noncomputable real
116:   $\sigma_{\mathrm{RO}}^*>0$ such that, for every $\sigma>0$,
117:   \[
118:     \sigma_{\mathrm{RO}}^*\leq\sigma
119:     \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
120:     \eqref{eq:release}\text{ is direct replace-one private for every such }
121:     \mathcal X,d,\text{ and oracle}.
122:   \]
123:   Privacy holds at $\sigma_{\mathrm{RO}}^*$.  This is a direct signed
124:   replacement result, not a two-step add/remove composition.
125: \end{enumerate}
126: \end{theorem}
127: 
128: \paragraph{Proof idea.}
129: The add/remove comparison is obtained from one history-dependent but
130: schedule-blind Gaussian decoder, so it preserves the exact hidden
131: fixed-cardinality mixture.  The finite procedure verifies rational upper
132: bounds for that mixture.  Conversely, a constant one-dimensional oracle
133: recovers the formal mixture by measurable projection.  Direct replacement
134: cannot use the same ordinary postprocessing argument: its positive and
135: negative shifts are compared by a normalized signed Gaussian interpolation,
136: then attained by a two-record constant oracle.  Continuity, Gaussian
137: degradation, a feasible fallback, and a small-noise separating event turn both
138: exact profile feasible sets into attained upper rays.

140: \section{Six conventional lemmas}
141: 
142: \begin{lemma}[Hockey-stick calculus]\label{lem:hs}
143: For a Markov kernel $K$,
144: \[
145:  H_\varepsilon(PK,QK)\leq H_\varepsilon(P,Q).
146: \]
147: If $\alpha_h\geq0$ and $\sum_h\alpha_h=1$, then
148: \[
149:  H_\varepsilon\!\left(\sum_h\alpha_hP_h,\sum_h\alpha_hQ_h\right)
150:  \leq\sum_h\alpha_hH_\varepsilon(P_h,Q_h).
151: \]
152: \end{lemma}
153: 
154: \begin{proof}
155: For an output test $0\leq f\leq1$, the function
156: $Kf(x)=\int f(y)K(x,dy)$ is an input test in $[0,1]$, which proves data
157: processing after taking the supremum over $f$.  For mixtures, expand the
158: difference of expectations as the common-weight sum of the component
159: differences, bound every summand by its hockey-stick divergence, and again
160: take the supremum.
161: \end{proof}
162: 
163: \begin{lemma}[Adaptive bounded Gaussian channel]\label{lem:channel}
164: If $\|v\|_2\leq1$ and $\sigma>0$, there is a channel, jointly measurable in
165: $v$ and its input, that sends
166: \[
167:  N(1,\sigma^2)\mapsto N(v,\sigma^2I_d),
168:  \qquad
169:  N(0,\sigma^2)\mapsto N(0,\sigma^2I_d).
170: \]
171: Consequently, if $v_t$ is a measurable function of the already decoded
172: history, these channels may be composed recursively into one common adaptive
173: decoder.
174: \end{lemma}
175: 
176: \begin{proof}
177: Pad $v$ to the unit vector
178: \[
179:  \widehat v=(\sqrt{1-\|v\|_2^2},v)\in\R^{d+1}.
180: \]
181: Apply a measurable Householder isometry carrying the first coordinate vector
182: to $\widehat v$ to $(X,Z)$, where
183: $Z\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ is independent, and discard coordinate zero.
184: Rotational invariance gives covariance $\sigma^2I_d$ in either case; the
185: retained mean is $v$ when $\mathbb EX=1$ and zero when $\mathbb EX=0$.
186: Recursively, decoded releases determine the next history and hence the next
187: $v_t$, so finite induction proves the asserted path-law identities.  The same
188: decoder is used on both privacy sides.
189: \end{proof}
190: 
191: \begin{lemma}[Exact hidden-schedule add/remove reduction]\label{lem:ar-upper}
192: Define
193: \[
194:  P_\sigma^+
195:  =\binom Tk^{-1}\sum_{|S|=k}N(\mathbf1_S,\sigma^2I_T),
196:  \qquad
197:  P_\sigma^0=N(0,\sigma^2I_T),
198: \]
199: and let $\Pi_{\mathrm{AR}}(\sigma)$ be the maximum of the two directed
200: hockey-stick divergences between them.  If
201: $\Pi_{\mathrm{AR}}(\sigma)\leq\delta$, then \eqref{eq:release} is
202: $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-private under add/remove adjacency for every admissible
203: oracle.
204: \end{lemma}
205: 
206: \begin{proof}
207: Write the adjacent datasets as $C$ and $C\cup\{u\}$, and condition on the
208: schedules of the common users.  Their adaptive aggregate is then common to
209: both sides.  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:channel} at time $t$ with
210: $v_t=\clip(q_t(h_{<t},u))$.  The resulting decoder depends on the common-user
211: assignment and the decoded history, but neither on the privacy side nor on
212: $u$'s hidden schedule.
213: 
214: For a temporarily fixed $S$, induction over time shows that a scalar path with
215: law $N(\mathbf1_S,\sigma^2I_T)$ decodes to the conditional transcript on
216: $C\cup\{u\}$, whereas a centered scalar path decodes to the conditional
217: transcript on $C$, independently of $S$.  Therefore the \emph{single}
218: schedule-blind decoder maps the whole pair $(P_\sigma^+,P_\sigma^0)$ to the
219: two conditional transcript laws.  Data processing applies before any
220: componentwise privacy bound is taken.  Finally mix over the common-user
221: schedules, whose weights agree on both sides, and repeat with the directions
222: reversed.  In particular, we never average fixed-$S$ divergences; doing so
223: would discard hidden-schedule amplification.
224: \end{proof}

226: \begin{lemma}[Finite verified calibration]\label{lem:finite}
227: For every rational request in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, a finite rational
228: checker constructs a positive fallback, rechecks every generated candidate,
229: and returns the least accepted scale among the fallback and a finite public
230: grid generated from the request.  Its returned scale satisfies
231: $\Pi_{\mathrm{AR}}(\widehat\sigma)\leq\delta$.
232: \end{lemma}
233: 
234: \begin{proof}
235: At a candidate $\sigma>0$, the exact fixed-cardinality experiment is bounded
236: by a two-sided scalar Gaussian profile at the conservative ratio
237: $\mu=k/\sigma$.  The checker accepts only finite rational witnesses whose
238: tail and exponential inequalities imply that this profile is at most
239: $\delta$.  Independently of search success, it uses
240: \[
241:  \sigma_{\mathrm{fb}}=\frac{k+1}{\delta}>0,
242:  \qquad
243:  \frac12\,\frac{k}{\sigma_{\mathrm{fb}}}
244:  =\frac{k\delta}{2(k+1)}\leq\delta,
245: \]
246: together with the verified Gaussian bound $\operatorname{GDP}(\mu,\varepsilon)
247: \leq\mu/2$.  Thus the fallback is sound.  A finite fold starts from it,
248: retains only rechecked candidates, and replaces the current candidate only by
249: a smaller accepted one.  Induction proves soundness throughout, and complete
250: enumeration proves minimum only within this accepted finite family.
251: \end{proof}

253: \begin{lemma}[Signed direct-replacement domination]\label{lem:signed}
254: Let a finite hidden family have scalar amplitudes $a_h$, vector means $m_h$,
255: and signed coefficients $c_h$, with
256: \[
257:  \|m_h\|_2\leq|a_h|\quad\text{for every }h.
258: \]
259: If all $c_ha_h$ are nonnegative, or all are nonpositive, then the Gaussian
260: stop loss of the vector family is no larger than that of the aligned scalar
261: family.  The comparison remains valid sequentially when positive conditional
262: likelihood factors from future adaptive releases multiply the $c_h$.
263: \end{lemma}
264: 
265: \begin{proof}
266: First normalize by the positive noise scale so the reference covariance is
267: the identity.  Smooth $x\mapsto(x-r)_+$ and interpolate from the normalized
268: vector means to scalar means on one Gaussian axis.  Gaussian integration by
269: parts expresses the derivative as a nonnegative smoothing factor times
270: \[
271:  \sum_{h,g}W_hW_g
272:  \bigl(a_ha_g-\langle m_h,m_g\rangle\bigr),
273: \]
274: where $W_h$ is $c_h$ multiplied by positive likelihood factors.  Hence the
275: $W_ha_h$ retain one common sign.  Moreover,
276: \[
277:  |W_hW_g\langle m_h,m_g\rangle|
278:  \leq |W_ha_h|\,|W_ga_g|,
279: \]
280: so every compensated contribution, and therefore the derivative, is
281: nonnegative.  Let the smoothing vanish.  For an adaptive path, apply the
282: one-step result backward from the terminal stop loss; future likelihood
283: factors are positive and preserve alignment at each step.
284: \end{proof}

286: \begin{lemma}[Lower realization and attained upper ray]\label{lem:threshold}
287: For both the add/remove and direct replace-one formal profiles:
288: \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.2em]
289: \item uniform privacy of all admissible oracles implies formal-profile
290:   feasibility;
291: \item on $0<k\leq T$ and $0<\delta<1$, the feasible positive scales form an
292:   upper ray $[\sigma^*,\infty)$ with $\sigma^*>0$ and feasibility at
293:   $\sigma^*$.
294: \end{enumerate}
295: \end{lemma}
296: 
297: \begin{proof}
298: For add/remove, take $d=1$ and the constant contribution $+1$.  Measurably
299: project the released scalar coordinate from the history-valued transcript.
300: The singleton-dataset and empty-dataset pushforwards are exactly
301: $P_\sigma^+$ and $P_\sigma^0$.  For replacement, use two distinct records
302: with constant contributions $+1$ and $-1$; projection of their singleton
303: transcripts gives the exact positive and negative formal laws.  Thus these
304: formal experiments are recovered by measurable projection, rather than being
305: identified with the raw history-valued transcript.
306: 
307: For either exact profile $\Pi$, Gaussian degradation makes $\Pi$ nonincreasing
308: in $\sigma$, and its finite Gaussian-mixture formula is continuous on
309: $(0,\infty)$.  A finite upper bound supplies a feasible scale.  Since $k>0$,
310: the source and reference means separate as $\sigma\downarrow0$; because
311: $\delta<1$, choose $\sigma_0>0$ with
312: $\Pi(\sigma_0)>\delta$.  Monotonicity makes every
313: $0<\sigma\leq\sigma_0$ infeasible.  The \emph{truncated} set
314: \[
315:  F^0=\{\sigma\in\R:\sigma_0\leq\sigma,\ \Pi(\sigma)\leq\delta\}
316: \]
317: is nonempty, closed, and bounded below, so its infimum $\sigma^*$ belongs to
318: $F^0$.  It is positive and feasible.  The definition of the infimum below
319: $\sigma^*$ and degradation above it show, for every $\sigma>0$,
320: \[
321:  \Pi(\sigma)\leq\delta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sigma^*\leq\sigma.
322: \]
323: \end{proof}

325: \section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}
326: 
327: \paragraph{Clause (i).}
328: Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} gives a checked positive rational scale with
329: $\Pi_{\mathrm{AR}}(\widehat\sigma)\leq\delta$ and proves its stated
330: finite-family leastness.  Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-upper} transports that exact
331: formal guarantee to every adaptive clipped transcript, in both privacy
332: directions.  This proves the finite add/remove clause.
333: 
334: \paragraph{Clause (ii).}
335: Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-upper} gives formal feasibility $\Rightarrow$ uniform
336: add/remove privacy.  The constant-$+1$ projection in
337: Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold} gives the converse, hence
338: \[
339:  \text{uniform add/remove privacy at }\sigma
340:  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
341:  \sigma>0\ \text{and}\ \Pi_{\mathrm{AR}}(\sigma)\leq\delta.
342: \]
343: The upper-ray part of that lemma supplies the noncomputable attained least
344: scale $\sigma_{\mathrm{AR}}^*$.
345: 
346: \paragraph{Clause (iii).}
347: For replacement, draw the removed and inserted users' schedules independently.
348: After conditioning on common-user schedules and dividing each innovation by
349: $\sigma$, source components have scalar amplitudes $+1/\sigma$ and reference
350: components have amplitudes $-1/\sigma$; unselected components have amplitude
351: zero.  Clipping gives the corresponding normalized vector bound
352: $\|m_h\|_2\leq|a_h|$.
353: 
354: In the forward hockey-stick expression, source coefficients are positive and
355: reference coefficients are multiplied by $-e^\varepsilon$; hence
356: $c_ha_h\geq0$.  In the reverse expression the signs swap and
357: $c_ha_h\leq0$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:signed}, applied backward through the adaptive
358: path, therefore bounds both actual directions by the exact scalar pair
359: \[
360:  P_{\sigma,\mathrm{RO}}^+
361:  =\binom Tk^{-1}\sum_{|S|=k}N(\mathbf1_S,\sigma^2I_T),
362:  \qquad
363:  P_{\sigma,\mathrm{RO}}^-
364:  =\binom Tk^{-1}\sum_{|S|=k}N(-\mathbf1_S,\sigma^2I_T).
365: \]
366: Only after this signed likelihood comparison do we decode the common
367: normalized innovation path into the transcript.  That decoder receives the
368: common-user assignment, public zero initial history, oracle, and $\sigma$, but
369: not the privacy side or either hidden replacement schedule.  Integrating the
370: unused schedule and mixing over common-user assignments preserves the bound.
371: 
372: The two-record $+1/-1$ projection in Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold} proves the
373: converse.  Thus uniform direct replace-one privacy is equivalent to feasibility
374: of the exact two-sided profile of
375: $(P_{\sigma,\mathrm{RO}}^+,P_{\sigma,\mathrm{RO}}^-)$.  Its attained-upper-ray
376: conclusion yields the noncomputable least scale $\sigma_{\mathrm{RO}}^*$.
377: \qed

379: \section{Degenerate cases, scope, and provenance}
380: 
381: The finite add/remove procedure deliberately allows $k=0$ and
382: $\delta\geq1$.  If $k=0$, no user ever contributes, so every positive scale
383: has a data-independent transcript.  If $\delta\geq1$, every hockey-stick
384: divergence is at most $1\leq\delta$, so again every positive scale is feasible.
385: In either case the feasible set is $(0,\infty)$ and has infimum zero but no
386: least positive member.  The calibrator remains sound and returns a positive
387: fallback; only the claim of an attained \emph{positive} global threshold
388: would be false.  This is exactly why clauses (ii) and (iii) assume
389: $k>0$ and $\delta<1$.  The case $\delta=0$ lies outside the total-calibrator
390: domain: its fallback divides by $\delta$, and a nonzero Gaussian shift need
391: not admit any finite pure-DP scale.
392: 
393: The theorem concerns independent per-user uniform fixed-cardinality
394: schedules, unit internal clipping, measurable adaptive queries, zero initial
395: history, and ideal real Gaussian noise.  Measurable postprocessing of the
396: transcript inherits the privacy guarantee.  It does not establish conformance
397: of a sampler, finite-precision Gaussian generator, Python/PyTorch/Opacus
398: implementation, distributed execution, or experiment.  The finite rational
399: calibrator is add/remove only and grid-relative; the exact uniform thresholds
400: are noncomputable and may exceed what a particular fixed oracle needs.

40: \begin{abstract}
41: We prove finite lower and upper bounds for the exact two-sided privacy
42: profile of a product of independent Poisson-sampled Gaussian releases.  The
43: lower bound evaluates one maximum-coordinate event.  The upper bound separates
44: the same product space into a below-threshold box and its complement.  The
45: box contribution is controlled by exact first and second moments of the
46: likelihood ratio, while the complement is charged by its source probability.
47: The reverse privacy direction follows from an exact likelihood identity and
48: a pointwise likelihood floor.  Every threshold is supplied rather than
49: optimized, so the result is finite, explicit, and directly suitable for
50: certified numerical evaluation.  A strict lower certificate and a non-strict
51: upper certificate then enclose the minimally calibrated noise standard
52: deviation.  The result turns a high-dimensional privacy calculation into a
53: finite collection of one-dimensional Gaussian evaluations while keeping both
54: privacy directions explicit.
55: \end{abstract}
56: 
57: \section{Why a finite bracket is useful}
58: 
59: Noise calibration asks a concrete question: for fixed
60: $(T,q,\Delta,\varepsilon,\delta)$, how large must the Gaussian standard
61: deviation be?  The exact answer is defined by a privacy profile on
62: $\R^T$.  Ordinary floating-point accountant outputs approximate this
63: profile, while asymptotic formulas describe its limiting scale; without a
64: directed error analysis, neither alone certifies that one particular finite
65: noise level is sufficient or insufficient.  Rigorous FFT and saddle-point
66: accountants show that such error control is possible
67: \cite{KoskelaEtAl2021,AlghamdiEtAl2023}.  Our aim here is a simpler finite
68: certificate built from supplied thresholds and one-dimensional Gaussian
69: quantities.
70: 
71: The bounds below provide precisely those two certificates.  A lower witness
72: larger than $\delta$ proves that a candidate standard deviation is too small.
73: An upper bound no larger than $\delta$ proves that another candidate is large
74: enough.  Together they enclose the minimum calibrated noise:
75: \[
76:   \boxed{
77:   \begin{array}{c}
78:     \text{lower profile certificate}>\delta
79:       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
80:       \text{candidate noise is insufficient},\\[1mm]
81:     \text{upper profile certificate}\leq\delta
82:       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
83:       \text{candidate noise is sufficient}.
84:   \end{array}}
85: \]
86: 
87: Both certificates come from one geometric split.  For a threshold $a$, let
88: \[
89:   B_a=\{x:x_i\leq a\text{ for every }i\},
90:   \qquad
91:   A_a=B_a^c=\{x:\max_i x_i>a\}.
92: \]
93: The rare-event region $A_a$ supplies the lower witness.  For the upper bound,
94: the same region is charged by its exact source probability, while the
95: complementary box $B_a$ is controlled by exact likelihood moments.  Figure
96: \ref{fig:box-split} summarizes the split.
97:

133: \section{Setup and main result}
134: 
135: Fix a horizon $T\in\N$, a sampling rate $q\in[0,1]$, a shift
136: $\Delta\in\R$, a profile parameter $\varepsilon\in\R$, and a noise standard
137: deviation $\sigma>0$.  The algebraic bracket is valid for every real
138: $\varepsilon$; the privacy-calibration corollary later assumes
139: $\varepsilon\geq0$.  In one coordinate, the reference and source laws are
140: respectively
141: \[
142:   Q_{\sigma,1}=\Normal(0,\sigma^2),
143:   \qquad
144:   P_{\sigma,1}=(1-q)\Normal(0,\sigma^2)
145:        +q\Normal(\Delta,\sigma^2).
146: \]
147: Across the $T$ coordinates, all draws are independent:
148: \[
149:   Q_\sigma=Q_{\sigma,1}^{\otimes T},
150:   \qquad
151:   P_\sigma=P_{\sigma,1}^{\otimes T}.
152: \]
153: 
154: For probability measures $P,Q$ and a real number $\xi$, define the directed
155: hockey-stick profile
156: \[
157:   H_\xi(P\Vert Q)
158:   =\sup_A\pos{P(A)-e^\xi Q(A)},
159: \]
160: where the supremum is over measurable events.  The exact two-sided profile is
161: \[
162:   \Pi_\varepsilon(P,Q)
163:   =\max\{H_\varepsilon(P\Vert Q),H_\varepsilon(Q\Vert P)\}.
164: \]
165: For the sampled Gaussian product, write
166: \[
167:   \Pi_\varepsilon(\sigma)=\Pi_\varepsilon(P_\sigma,Q_\sigma).
168: \]
169: At $\sigma=0$, this notation means the same product construction with
170: degenerate Gaussians of variance zero.  Thus the exact profile is defined on
171: all $\sigma\geq0$, although the closed formulas below require $\sigma>0$
172: because they use the standardized shift $\Delta/\sigma$.
173: 
174: The positive rescaling $x\mapsto x/\sigma$ preserves every event probability
175: and hence preserves the profile.  We may therefore work with
176: \[
177:   \mu=\frac{\Delta}{\sigma},
178:   \qquad
179:   Q=\Normal(0,1)^{\otimes T},
180:   \qquad
181:   P=\bigl[(1-q)\Normal(0,1)+q\Normal(\mu,1)\bigr]^{\otimes T}.
182: \]
183: Let $\Phi$ denote the standard normal distribution function.  For a threshold
184: $a\in\R$, set
185: \begin{align}
186:   p_P(a)&=\bigl[(1-q)\Phi(a)+q\Phi(a-\mu)\bigr]^T,\label{eq:source-box-mass}\\
187:   p_Q(a)&=\Phi(a)^T,\label{eq:reference-box-mass}\\
188:   m_2(a)&=\Bigl[(1-q)^2\Phi(a)
189:       +2q(1-q)\Phi(a-\mu)
190:       +q^2e^{\mu^2}\Phi(a-2\mu)\Bigr]^T.\label{eq:second-moment}
191: \end{align}
192: Thus $p_P(a)$ and $p_Q(a)$ will be the source and reference probabilities of the
193: below-threshold box, while $m_2(a)$ will be a truncated second moment.
194: 
195: For any real $\xi$, define
196: \begin{align}
197:   v_\xi(a)
198:     &=m_2(a)-2e^\xi p_P(a)+e^{2\xi}p_Q(a),\label{eq:centered-moment}\\
199:   R_\xi(a)
200:     &=\frac{\sqrt{p_Q(a)v_\xi(a)}+p_P(a)-e^\xi p_Q(a)}{2},\label{eq:residual-bound}\\
201:   U_{\mathrm F}(\xi,a)
202:     &=1-p_P(a)+\min\{p_P(a),R_\xi(a)\}.\label{eq:forward-upper}
203: \end{align}
204: The proof below shows in particular that $v_\xi(a)\geq0$ and that the
205: right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:forward-upper} is nonnegative.
206: 
207: For the reverse direction, define
208: \begin{align}
209:   U_{\mathrm R}^{\mathrm{dual}}(\varepsilon,a)
210:     &=\pos{1-e^\varepsilon
211:         +e^\varepsilon U_{\mathrm F}(-\varepsilon,a)},\label{eq:reverse-dual}\\
212:   U_{\mathrm R}^{\mathrm{floor}}(\varepsilon)
213:     &=\pos{1-e^\varepsilon(1-q)^T},\label{eq:reverse-floor}\\
214:   U_{\mathrm R}(\varepsilon,a)
215:     &=\min\bigl\{U_{\mathrm R}^{\mathrm{dual}}(\varepsilon,a),
216:                   U_{\mathrm R}^{\mathrm{floor}}(\varepsilon)\bigr\}.
217: \end{align}
218: Finally, define the maximum-event lower expression
219: \begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-expression}
220:   L(\varepsilon,a)
221:   =\pos{1-p_P(a)-e^\varepsilon\bigl(1-p_Q(a)\bigr)}.
222: \end{equation}
223: 
224: \begin{theorem}[Finite supplied-threshold bracket]\label{thm:finite-bracket}
225: For every three thresholds $a_{\mathrm L},a_{\mathrm F},a_{\mathrm R}\in\R$,
226: \begin{equation}\label{eq:main-bracket}
227:   L(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm L})
228:   \leq \Pi_\varepsilon(\sigma)
229:   \leq
230:   \max\bigl\{
231:     U_{\mathrm F}(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm F}),
232:     U_{\mathrm R}(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm R})
233:   \bigr\}.
234: \end{equation}
235: The thresholds are arbitrary and need not be equal.  The conclusion includes
236: $T=0$ and the endpoint rates $q=0$ and $q=1$.
237: \end{theorem}
238: 
239: \paragraph{How to read the three thresholds.}
240: The lower threshold $a_{\mathrm L}$ chooses one event whose privacy loss is
241: evaluated exactly.  The forward threshold $a_{\mathrm F}$ balances the exact
242: source mass outside the box against the moment estimate inside it.  The
243: reverse threshold $a_{\mathrm R}$ performs the same balance for the
244: reciprocal forward profile used in the reverse direction.  The second reverse
245: bound, based on the likelihood floor, has no threshold and can be preferable
246: when its pointwise information is already decisive.  The theorem permits all
247: three supplied thresholds to differ but makes no claim that any of them is
248: optimal.
249: 
250: \paragraph{Proof idea.}
251: The lower bound evaluates the profile at the event that at least one
252: coordinate exceeds a threshold.  For the forward upper bound, we split the
253: likelihood-ratio integral into the below-threshold box and its complement.
254: The complement costs at most its source probability.  On the box, exact
255: first and second moments and Cauchy--Schwarz give the displayed residual.
256: The reverse direction is bounded in two independent ways: by an exact
257: identity relating it to the forward profile at $-\varepsilon$, and by the
258: fact that the likelihood ratio is never smaller than $(1-q)^T$.
259: 
260: \section{Likelihood and truncated moments}
261: 
262: Let $Z\sim\Normal(0,1)$.  The one-coordinate likelihood ratio of the
263: normalized source with respect to the normalized reference is
264: \begin{equation}\label{eq:one-likelihood}
265:   \ell(z)
266:   =(1-q)+q\exp\!\left(\mu z-\frac{\mu^2}{2}\right).
267: \end{equation}
268: Indeed, the density ratio of $\Normal(\mu,1)$ against $\Normal(0,1)$ is the
269: exponential factor in~\eqref{eq:one-likelihood}.  Hence the product
270: likelihood ratio is
271: \begin{equation}\label{eq:product-likelihood}
272:   \Lambda(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{T}\ell(x_i),
273:   \qquad x\in\R^T.
274: \end{equation}
275: It is positive, integrable under $Q$, and $\E_Q\Lambda=1$.
276: 
277: For a threshold $a$, introduce the box
278: \[
279:   B_a=\{x\in\R^T:x_i\leq a\text{ for every }i\}
280: \]
281: and its complement
282: \[
283:   A_a=B_a^c=\{x\in\R^T:\max_i x_i>a\}.
284: \]
285: 
286: \begin{lemma}[Exact truncated moments]\label{lem:truncated-moments}
287: For every $a\in\R$,
288: \begin{align}
289:   Q(B_a)&=p_Q(a),\\
290:   \E_Q[\Lambda\mathbf 1_{B_a}]&=p_P(a),\\
291:   \E_Q[\Lambda^2\mathbf 1_{B_a}]&=m_2(a),\\
292:   \E_Q[(\Lambda-e^\xi)^2\mathbf 1_{B_a}]&=v_\xi(a).
293: \end{align}
294: In particular, $v_\xi(a)\geq0$.
295: \end{lemma}
296: 
297: \begin{proof}
298: Independence gives $Q(B_a)=\Phi(a)^T=p_Q(a)$.  The standard Gaussian tilt
299: identity
300: \begin{equation}\label{eq:tilt}
301:   \E\left[e^{tZ-t^2/2}\mathbf 1_{\{Z\leq a\}}\right]
302:   =\Phi(a-t)
303: \end{equation}
304: implies
305: \[
306:   \E[\ell(Z)\mathbf 1_{\{Z\leq a\}}]
307:   =(1-q)\Phi(a)+q\Phi(a-\mu).
308: \]
309: Taking the $T$-fold product gives the second displayed identity and
310: formula~\eqref{eq:source-box-mass}.
311: 
312: Squaring~\eqref{eq:one-likelihood} yields
313: \[
314:   \ell(z)^2
315:   =(1-q)^2
316:    +2q(1-q)e^{\mu z-\mu^2/2}
317:    +q^2e^{2\mu z-\mu^2}.
318: \]
319: The first tilted term is handled by~\eqref{eq:tilt} with $t=\mu$.  For the
320: last term, write
321: \[
322:   e^{2\mu z-\mu^2}
323:   =e^{\mu^2}e^{2\mu z-(2\mu)^2/2}
324: \]
325: and use~\eqref{eq:tilt} with $t=2\mu$.  This gives the one-coordinate second
326: moment
327: \[
328:   (1-q)^2\Phi(a)
329:   +2q(1-q)\Phi(a-\mu)
330:   +q^2e^{\mu^2}\Phi(a-2\mu).
331: \]
332: Independence gives~\eqref{eq:second-moment}.  Finally, expanding the square
333: inside the integral gives
334: \[
335:   \E_Q[(\Lambda-e^\xi)^2\mathbf 1_{B_a}]
336:   =m_2(a)-2e^\xi p_P(a)+e^{2\xi}p_Q(a)=v_\xi(a).
337: \]
338: It is nonnegative because it is the expectation of a square.
339: \end{proof}
340: 
341: \section{Proof of the finite bracket}
342: 
343: \subsection{Maximum-event lower bound}
344: 
345: Under the reference law,
346: \[
347:   Q(A_a)=1-p_Q(a).
348: \]
349: Under the source law, one coordinate lies below $a$ with probability
350: $(1-q)\Phi(a)+q\Phi(a-\mu)$, and therefore
351: \[
352:   P(A_a)=1-p_P(a).
353: \]
354: Evaluating the supremum defining the forward profile at $A_a$ gives
355: \[
356:   H_\varepsilon(P\Vert Q)
357:   \geq
358:   \pos{P(A_a)-e^\varepsilon Q(A_a)}
359:   =L(\varepsilon,a).
360: \]
361: Since $\Pi_\varepsilon(\sigma)$ is at least its forward direction, this proves the lower
362: bound in~\eqref{eq:main-bracket} at $a=a_{\mathrm L}$.
363: 
364: \subsection{Forward upper bound}
365: 
366: Put $\alpha=e^\xi$.  Since $P$ has likelihood ratio $\Lambda$ with respect
367: to $Q$,
368: \[
369:   H_\xi(P\Vert Q)=\E_Q[(\Lambda-\alpha)_+].
370: \]
371: Split this expectation over $B_a$ and $A_a$.  On $A_a$,
372: \[
373:   (\Lambda-\alpha)_+\leq\Lambda,
374: \]
375: so
376: \begin{equation}\label{eq:outside-bound}
377:   \E_Q[(\Lambda-\alpha)_+\mathbf 1_{A_a}]
378:   \leq \E_Q[\Lambda\mathbf 1_{A_a}]
379:   =1-p_P(a).
380: \end{equation}
381: 
382: On $B_a$, use $x_+=(|x|+x)/2$ and Cauchy--Schwarz:
383: \begin{align*}
384:   \E_Q[(\Lambda-\alpha)_+\mathbf 1_{B_a}]
385:   &=\frac12\left(
386:       \E_Q[|\Lambda-\alpha|\mathbf 1_{B_a}]
387:       +p_P(a)-\alpha p_Q(a)
388:     \right)\\
389:   &\leq\frac12\left(
390:       \sqrt{Q(B_a)\E_Q[(\Lambda-\alpha)^2\mathbf 1_{B_a}]}
391:       +p_P(a)-\alpha p_Q(a)
392:     \right)\\
393:   &=R_\xi(a).
394: \end{align*}
395: There is also the simpler bound
396: \[
397:   \E_Q[(\Lambda-\alpha)_+\mathbf 1_{B_a}]
398:   \leq\E_Q[\Lambda\mathbf 1_{B_a}]=p_P(a).
399: \]
400: Adding~\eqref{eq:outside-bound} and taking the smaller of the two box bounds
401: gives
402: \begin{equation}\label{eq:forward-result}
403:   H_\xi(P\Vert Q)\leq U_{\mathrm F}(\xi,a).
404: \end{equation}
405: This also shows directly that the right-hand side is nonnegative.
406: 
407: \subsection{Reverse upper bounds}
408: 
409: For any nonnegative likelihood ratio $\Lambda$ with $\E_Q\Lambda=1$ and any
410: $\alpha>0$, one has the exact identity
411: \begin{equation}\label{eq:duality}
412:   \E_Q[(1-\alpha\Lambda)_+]
413:   =1-\alpha+\alpha\E_Q[(\Lambda-\alpha^{-1})_+].
414: \end{equation}
415: To verify it, apply
416: \[
417:   x_+-(-x)_+=x
418: \]
419: to $x=\alpha\Lambda-1$ and then take expectations.  With
420: $\alpha=e^\varepsilon$, identity~\eqref{eq:duality} becomes
421: \begin{equation}\label{eq:profile-duality}
422:   H_\varepsilon(Q\Vert P)
423:   =1-e^\varepsilon
424:     +e^\varepsilon H_{-\varepsilon}(P\Vert Q).
425: \end{equation}
426: Applying~\eqref{eq:forward-result} at $-\varepsilon$ proves
427: \[
428:   H_\varepsilon(Q\Vert P)
429:   \leq U_{\mathrm R}^{\mathrm{dual}}(\varepsilon,a).
430: \]
431: Since the untruncated expression upper-bounds the nonnegative reverse profile,
432: it is itself nonnegative.  The positive-part operation therefore leaves it
433: unchanged.
434: 
435: There is a second reverse bound.  From~\eqref{eq:one-likelihood},
436: \[
437:   \ell(z)\geq1-q,
438: \]
439: and hence~\eqref{eq:product-likelihood} gives
440: \[
441:   \Lambda(x)\geq(1-q)^T
442:   \qquad\text{for every }x.
443: \]
444: Therefore
445: \begin{align*}
446:   H_\varepsilon(Q\Vert P)
447:   &=\E_Q[(1-e^\varepsilon\Lambda)_+]\\
448:   &\leq\pos{1-e^\varepsilon(1-q)^T}
449:    =U_{\mathrm R}^{\mathrm{floor}}(\varepsilon).
450: \end{align*}
451: Taking the smaller of the two valid reverse bounds gives
452: \[
453:   H_\varepsilon(Q\Vert P)
454:   \leq U_{\mathrm R}(\varepsilon,a).
455: \]
456: Combining this with~\eqref{eq:forward-result} at $\xi=\varepsilon$ and taking
457: the maximum of the two directed profiles proves the upper bound
458: in~\eqref{eq:main-bracket}.  This completes the proof of
459: Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-bracket}.
460: 
461: \subsection{Degenerate finite cases}
462: 
463: If $T=0$, all product likelihoods and below-box masses equal one.  The source
464: and reference are the same law on the one-point zero-dimensional space, and
465: the displayed formulas reduce to the corresponding profile of two identical
466: laws.  If $q=0$, the source and reference again coincide.  If $q=1$, the
467: source is an ordinary shifted Gaussian product.  None of the preceding
468: arguments divides by $q$, by $T$, or by $1-q$, so all three cases are included.
469: The only required division is by the noise standard deviation in
470: $\mu=\Delta/\sigma$, and this is why the theorem assumes $\sigma>0$.
471: 
472: \section{Calibrated-noise consequence}
473: 
474: For the privacy interpretation, now assume $\varepsilon\geq0$ and fix a target
475: $\delta\in[0,1]$.  Define the feasible set
476: \[
477:   \mathcal S
478:   =\{\sigma\geq0:\Pi_\varepsilon(\sigma)\leq\delta\}.
479: \]
480: Let
481: \[
482:   \sigma_\star=\inf\mathcal S,
483: \]
484: whenever the feasible set is nonempty.  The upper certificate below proves
485: this nonemptiness as part of its conclusion.
486: 
487: \begin{corollary}[Certified calibration interval]\label{cor:calibration}
488: Let $\sigma_{\mathrm L},\sigma_{\mathrm U}>0$.  Choose a lower threshold
489: $a_{\mathrm L}$ for $\sigma_{\mathrm L}$ and forward and reverse upper
490: thresholds $a_{\mathrm F},a_{\mathrm R}$ for $\sigma_{\mathrm U}$.  In each
491: formula, use the corresponding standardized shift $\mu=\Delta/\sigma$.
492: If
493: \begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-certificate}
494:   \delta<L_{\sigma_{\mathrm L}}(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm L})
495: \end{equation}
496: and
497: \begin{equation}\label{eq:upper-certificate}
498:   \max\bigl\{
499:     U_{\mathrm F,\sigma_{\mathrm U}}(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm F}),
500:     U_{\mathrm R,\sigma_{\mathrm U}}(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm R})
501:   \bigr\}
502:   \leq\delta,
503: \end{equation}
504: then
505: \[
506:   \boxed{\sigma_{\mathrm L}\leq\sigma_\star\leq\sigma_{\mathrm U}.}
507: \]
508: No attainment assumption on $\sigma_\star$ is needed.
509: \end{corollary}
510: 
511: \begin{proof}
512: By Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-bracket}, condition~\eqref{eq:lower-certificate}
513: implies
514: \[
515:   \Pi_\varepsilon(\sigma_{\mathrm L})>\delta,
516: \]
517: so $\sigma_{\mathrm L}\notin\mathcal S$.  Condition~\eqref{eq:upper-certificate}
518: implies
519: \[
520:   \Pi_\varepsilon(\sigma_{\mathrm U})\leq\delta,
521: \]
522: so $\sigma_{\mathrm U}\in\mathcal S$.  In particular, $\mathcal S$ is
523: nonempty and $\sigma_\star\leq\sigma_{\mathrm U}$.
524: 
525: It remains to justify the lower endpoint.  The exact profile is nonincreasing
526: in $\sigma$: if $0\leq\sigma_1\leq\sigma_2$, one obtains both laws at noise
527: level $\sigma_2$ from the corresponding laws at level $\sigma_1$ by adding
528: the same independent centered Gaussian noise of variance
529: $\sigma_2^2-\sigma_1^2$.  Applying a common random transformation cannot
530: increase either directed privacy profile.  Thus $\mathcal S$ is upward
531: closed.  If some feasible $\sigma$ were smaller than
532: $\sigma_{\mathrm L}$, upward closure would make $\sigma_{\mathrm L}$ feasible,
533: contrary to the strict lower certificate.  Hence
534: $\sigma_{\mathrm L}\leq\sigma$ for every $\sigma\in\mathcal S$, and therefore
535: $\sigma_{\mathrm L}\leq\inf\mathcal S=\sigma_\star$.
536: \end{proof}
537: 
538: The formulas use finitely many Gaussian CDF values, exponentials, rational
539: operations, natural powers, and two square roots.  Directed rational
540: enclosures of these quantities therefore turn the bracket into a finite,
541: machine-checkable calibration certificate.  Appendix~\ref{sec:certified-evaluation}
542: gives the details.

544: \section{A finite numerical illustration}
545: 
546: To show the scale of the construction, consider the maintained diagnostic
547: row
548: \[
549:   T=100,\qquad q=0.0025,\qquad \Delta=1,
550:   \qquad \varepsilon=1,\qquad \delta=0.1.
551: \]
552: A direct threshold search in the finite formulas gives
553: \[
554: \begin{array}{c|c|c}
555: \text{candidate} & \text{supplied threshold} & \text{profile expression}\\
556: \hline
557: \sigma_{\mathrm L}=0.268600 & a_{\mathrm L}=3.669486
558:   & L(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm L})\approx0.1000288274\\
559: \sigma_{\mathrm U}=0.286000 & a_{\mathrm F}=4.020051
560:   & U_{\mathrm F}(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm F})\approx0.0994524972
561: \end{array}
562: \]
563: with reverse threshold $a_{\mathrm R}=3.454911$; on this row the forward
564: upper bound is the active direction.  The separately supplied PLD diagnostic
565: value is
566: \[
567:   \sigma_{\mathrm{PLD}}=0.2692892144.
568: \]
569: Using that diagnostic only as a scale, the displayed interval has relative
570: width
571: \[
572:   \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm U}-\sigma_{\mathrm L}}
573:        {\sigma_{\mathrm{PLD}}}
574:   \approx 6.46\%.
575: \]
576: 
577: These decimals illustrate the formulas and are not theorem authority.  A
578: formal certificate replaces them by outward rational intervals for every
579: Gaussian CDF, exponential, and square root, then checks the strict lower and
580: non-strict upper comparisons from Corollary~\ref{cor:calibration}.
581: 
582: \paragraph{Scope and limitations.}
583: The theorem gives a rigorous finite bracket for every supplied threshold.  It
584: does not assert that the maximum event is optimal, that either upper bound is
585: tight, or that a supplied threshold minimizes the formula.  It analyzes the
586: ideal independent Poisson-sampled Gaussian product laws, not shuffled
587: sampling, exact-size sampling, adaptive transcript privacy, or finite-precision
588: Gaussian generation.  The present benchmark shows that the lower bound is
589: often close while the upper bound can be loose; practical tightness is a
590: separate problem from correctness of the bracket.

646: \section{Certified evaluation}\label{sec:certified-evaluation}
647: 
648: For each occurrence of $\Phi(x)$ and $e^x$, supply a rational interval known
649: to contain the exact value.  Propagate these intervals outward through
650: addition, subtraction, multiplication of nonnegative quantities, and
651: natural-number powers.  For a square root, supply nonnegative rationals
652: $\ell,u$ and verify
653: \[
654:   \ell^2\leq z\leq u^2.
655: \]
656: Monotonicity of the square root then gives
657: \[
658:   \ell\leq\sqrt z\leq u.
659: \]
660: The resulting rational lower endpoint for~\eqref{eq:lower-expression} and
661: rational upper endpoint for the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:main-bracket}
662: are genuine bounds on the exact profile.  If they satisfy the strict and
663: non-strict comparisons in~\eqref{eq:lower-certificate} and
664: \eqref{eq:upper-certificate}, Corollary~\ref{cor:calibration} applies.
665: 
666: An external threshold optimizer or floating-point accountant may propose
667: thresholds and rational intervals, but it is not part of the proof.  The
668: mathematical conclusion follows only after the displayed containments and
669: inequalities have been verified.
