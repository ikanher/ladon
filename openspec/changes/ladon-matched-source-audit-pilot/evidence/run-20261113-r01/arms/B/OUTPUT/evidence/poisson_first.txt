\title{Finite Noise Certification for Poisson-Sampled Gaussian Products}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove finite lower and upper bounds for the exact two-sided privacy
profile of a product of independent Poisson-sampled Gaussian releases.  The
lower bound evaluates one maximum-coordinate event.  The upper bound separates
the same product space into a below-threshold box and its complement.  The
box contribution is controlled by exact first and second moments of the
likelihood ratio, while the complement is charged by its source probability.
The reverse privacy direction follows from an exact likelihood identity and
a pointwise likelihood floor.  Every threshold is supplied rather than
optimized, so the result is finite, explicit, and directly suitable for
certified numerical evaluation.  A strict lower certificate and a non-strict
upper certificate then enclose the minimally calibrated noise standard
deviation.  The result turns a high-dimensional privacy calculation into a
finite collection of one-dimensional Gaussian evaluations while keeping both
privacy directions explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why a finite bracket is useful}

Noise calibration asks a concrete question: for fixed
$(T,q,\Delta,\varepsilon,\delta)$, how large must the Gaussian standard
deviation be?  The exact answer is defined by a privacy profile on
$\R^T$.  Ordinary floating-point accountant outputs approximate this
profile, while asymptotic formulas describe its limiting scale; without a
directed error analysis, neither alone certifies that one particular finite
noise level is sufficient or insufficient.  Rigorous FFT and saddle-point
accountants show that such error control is possible
\cite{KoskelaEtAl2021,AlghamdiEtAl2023}.  Our aim here is a simpler finite
certificate built from supplied thresholds and one-dimensional Gaussian
quantities.

The bounds below provide precisely those two certificates.  A lower witness
larger than $\delta$ proves that a candidate standard deviation is too small.
An upper bound no larger than $\delta$ proves that another candidate is large
enough.  Together they enclose the minimum calibrated noise:
\[
  \boxed{
  \begin{array}{c}
    \text{lower profile certificate}>\delta
      \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
      \text{candidate noise is insufficient},\\[1mm]
    \text{upper profile certificate}\leq\delta
      \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
      \text{candidate noise is sufficient}.
  \end{array}}
\]

Both certificates come from one geometric split.  For a threshold $a$, let
\[
  B_a=\{x:x_i\leq a\text{ for every }i\},
  \qquad
  A_a=B_a^c=\{x:\max_i x_i>a\}.
\]
The rare-event region $A_a$ supplies the lower witness.  For the upper bound,
the same region is charged by its exact source probability, while the
complementary box $B_a$ is controlled by exact likelihood moments.  Figure
\ref{fig:box-split} summarizes the split.

This is the central idea of the theorem.  Threshold search may be performed
externally, but correctness of the resulting bracket depends only on the
finite formulas proved below.

\section{Setup and main result}

Fix a horizon $T\in\N$, a sampling rate $q\in[0,1]$, a shift
$\Delta\in\R$, a profile parameter $\varepsilon\in\R$, and a noise standard
deviation $\sigma>0$.  The algebraic bracket is valid for every real
$\varepsilon$; the privacy-calibration corollary later assumes
$\varepsilon\geq0$.  In one coordinate, the reference and source laws are
respectively
\[
  Q_{\sigma,1}=\Normal(0,\sigma^2),
  \qquad
  P_{\sigma,1}=(1-q)\Normal(0,\sigma^2)
       +q\Normal(\Delta,\sigma^2).
\]
Across the $T$ coordinates, all draws are independent:
\[
  Q_\sigma=Q_{\sigma,1}^{\otimes T},
  \qquad
  P_\sigma=P_{\sigma,1}^{\otimes T}.
\]

For probability measures $P,Q$ and a real number $\xi$, define the directed
hockey-stick profile
\[
  H_\xi(P\Vert Q)
  =\sup_A\pos{P(A)-e^\xi Q(A)},
\]
where the supremum is over measurable events.  The exact two-sided profile is
\[
  \Pi_\varepsilon(P,Q)
  =\max\{H_\varepsilon(P\Vert Q),H_\varepsilon(Q\Vert P)\}.
\]
For the sampled Gaussian product, write
\[
  \Pi_\varepsilon(\sigma)=\Pi_\varepsilon(P_\sigma,Q_\sigma).
\]
At $\sigma=0$, this notation means the same product construction with
degenerate Gaussians of variance zero.  Thus the exact profile is defined on
all $\sigma\geq0$, although the closed formulas below require $\sigma>0$
because they use the standardized shift $\Delta/\sigma$.

The positive rescaling $x\mapsto x/\sigma$ preserves every event probability
and hence preserves the profile.  We may therefore work with
\[
  \mu=\frac{\Delta}{\sigma},
  \qquad
  Q=\Normal(0,1)^{\otimes T},
  \qquad
  P=\bigl[(1-q)\Normal(0,1)+q\Normal(\mu,1)\bigr]^{\otimes T}.
\]
Let $\Phi$ denote the standard normal distribution function.  For a threshold
$a\in\R$, set
\begin{align}
  p_P(a)&=\bigl[(1-q)\Phi(a)+q\Phi(a-\mu)\bigr]^T,\label{eq:source-box-mass}\\
  p_Q(a)&=\Phi(a)^T,\label{eq:reference-box-mass}\\
  m_2(a)&=\Bigl[(1-q)^2\Phi(a)
      +2q(1-q)\Phi(a-\mu)
      +q^2e^{\mu^2}\Phi(a-2\mu)\Bigr]^T.\label{eq:second-moment}
\end{align}
Thus $p_P(a)$ and $p_Q(a)$ will be the source and reference probabilities of the
below-threshold box, while $m_2(a)$ will be a truncated second moment.

For any real $\xi$, define
\begin{align}
  v_\xi(a)
    &=m_2(a)-2e^\xi p_P(a)+e^{2\xi}p_Q(a),\label{eq:centered-moment}\\
  R_\xi(a)
    &=\frac{\sqrt{p_Q(a)v_\xi(a)}+p_P(a)-e^\xi p_Q(a)}{2},\label{eq:residual-bound}\\
  U_{\mathrm F}(\xi,a)
    &=1-p_P(a)+\min\{p_P(a),R_\xi(a)\}.\label{eq:forward-upper}
\end{align}
The proof below shows in particular that $v_\xi(a)\geq0$ and that the
right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:forward-upper} is nonnegative.

For the reverse direction, define
\begin{align}
  U_{\mathrm R}^{\mathrm{dual}}(\varepsilon,a)
    &=\pos{1-e^\varepsilon
        +e^\varepsilon U_{\mathrm F}(-\varepsilon,a)},\label{eq:reverse-dual}\\
  U_{\mathrm R}^{\mathrm{floor}}(\varepsilon)
    &=\pos{1-e^\varepsilon(1-q)^T},\label{eq:reverse-floor}\\
  U_{\mathrm R}(\varepsilon,a)
    &=\min\bigl\{U_{\mathrm R}^{\mathrm{dual}}(\varepsilon,a),
                  U_{\mathrm R}^{\mathrm{floor}}(\varepsilon)\bigr\}.
\end{align}
Finally, define the maximum-event lower expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-expression}
  L(\varepsilon,a)
  =\pos{1-p_P(a)-e^\varepsilon\bigl(1-p_Q(a)\bigr)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Finite supplied-threshold bracket]\label{thm:finite-bracket}
For every three thresholds $a_{\mathrm L},a_{\mathrm F},a_{\mathrm R}\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-bracket}
  L(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm L})
  \leq \Pi_\varepsilon(\sigma)
  \leq
  \max\bigl\{
    U_{\mathrm F}(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm F}),
    U_{\mathrm R}(\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm R})
  \bigr\}.
\end{equation}
The thresholds are arbitrary and need not be equal.  The conclusion includes
$T=0$ and the endpoint rates $q=0$ and $q=1$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{How to read the three thresholds.}
The lower threshold $a_{\mathrm L}$ chooses one event whose privacy loss is
evaluated exactly.  The forward threshold $a_{\mathrm F}$ balances the exact
source mass outside the box against the moment estimate inside it.  The
reverse threshold $a_{\mathrm R}$ performs the same balance for the
reciprocal forward profile used in the reverse direction.  The second reverse
bound, based on the likelihood floor, has no threshold and can be preferable
when its pointwise information is already decisive.  The theorem permits all
three supplied thresholds to differ but makes no claim that any of them is
optimal.

\paragraph{Proof idea.}
The lower bound evaluates the profile at the event that at least one
coordinate exceeds a threshold.  For the forward upper bound, we split the
likelihood-ratio integral into the below-threshold box and its complement.
The complement costs at most its source probability.  On the box, exact
first and second moments and Cauchy--Schwarz give the displayed residual.
The reverse direction is bounded in two independent ways: by an exact
identity relating it to the forward profile at $-\varepsilon$, and by the
fact that the likelihood ratio is never smaller than $(1-q)^T$.
