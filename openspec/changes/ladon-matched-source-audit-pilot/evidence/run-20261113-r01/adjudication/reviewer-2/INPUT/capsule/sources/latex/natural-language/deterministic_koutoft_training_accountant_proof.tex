\title{A Conventional Proof of the Deterministic\\
$k$-out-of-$T$ Training Accountant}
\author{}
\date{26 July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We give a conventional proof for an ideal adaptive training mechanism in
which each user independently receives a hidden, uniform $k$-subset of the
$T$ release positions.  The result has three parts: an executable
exact-rational add/remove calibration, an attained least scale for privacy
uniformly over all admissible adaptive oracles under add/remove adjacency, and
an attained least scale for direct replace-one privacy.  The finite
calibrator is add/remove only and is minimal only within its checked finite
candidate family.  The two exact thresholds are noncomputable real numbers.
Here ``deterministic'' describes the proof and calibration, not the randomized
hidden schedules; neither Monte Carlo estimation nor EVR is used.
The proof isolates the hidden-schedule mixture, the adaptive Gaussian channel,
the signed interpolation required for direct replacement, the lower
realizations, and the topology behind threshold attainment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Main theorem}

\paragraph{Setup and notation.}
Let $\mathcal X$ be a record type with decidable equality, so a dataset is a
finite set $D\subseteq\mathcal X$.  Fix $T>0$, $0\leq k\leq T$, an output
dimension $d$, and a measurable adaptive oracle.  From the previously released
history $h_{<t}$ and a record $u$, the oracle returns
$q_t(h_{<t},u)\in\R^d$.  Each user independently receives
\[
  S_u\sim\operatorname{Unif}
  \{S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,T-1\}:|S|=k\}.
\]
Starting from the \emph{zero initial history}, the ideal mechanism releases
\begin{equation}\label{eq:release}
  Y_t=\sum_{u\in D}\mathbf1_{\{t\in S_u\}}
       \clip\!\left(q_t(Y_{<t},u)\right)+\sigma Z_t,
  \qquad 0\leq t<T,
\end{equation}
where $\|\clip(v)\|_2\leq1$ and the $Z_t$ are independent standard
$d$-dimensional Gaussians.  Schedules are hidden and marginalized only after
the complete conditional transcript law is formed.

Add/remove adjacency inserts or removes one record.  Direct replace-one
adjacency compares
\[
 D_L=C\cup\{u\},\qquad D_R=C\cup\{v\},
 \qquad u\neq v,\quad u,v\notin C.
\]
For probability measures $P,Q$, write
\[
 H_\varepsilon(P,Q)
 =\sup_{0\leq f\leq1}
   \left(\int f\,dP-e^\varepsilon\int f\,dQ\right).
\]
Approximate differential privacy means that this quantity is at most
$\delta$ in both directions for every adjacent pair.

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic $k$-out-of-$T$ accountant]\label{thm:main}
Let $T,k\in\N$ and $\varepsilon,\delta\in\Q$ satisfy
\[
 0<T,\qquad k\leq T,\qquad 0\leq\varepsilon,\qquad 0<\delta.
\]
The following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item A terminating exact-rational procedure returns
  $\widehat\sigma\in\Q_{>0}$.  For every record type with decidable equality,
  every $d$, and every measurable adaptive oracle, \eqref{eq:release} at
  $\widehat\sigma$ is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-private for add/remove adjacency.
  The returned scale is the minimum among an independently checked total
  fallback and the accepted members of a finite public rational grid generated
  from the request.
  This is an add/remove theorem and a grid-relative minimum, not a direct
  replace-one calibration or a global minimum over $\R_{>0}$.

\item If additionally $0<k$ and $\delta<1$, there is a noncomputable real
  $\sigma_{\mathrm{AR}}^*>0$ such that, for every $\sigma>0$,
  \[
    \sigma_{\mathrm{AR}}^*\leq\sigma
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \eqref{eq:release}\text{ is add/remove private for every such }
    \mathcal X,d,\text{ and oracle}.
  \]
  Privacy holds at $\sigma_{\mathrm{AR}}^*$.

\item Under the same additional assumptions there is a noncomputable real
  $\sigma_{\mathrm{RO}}^*>0$ such that, for every $\sigma>0$,
  \[
    \sigma_{\mathrm{RO}}^*\leq\sigma
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \eqref{eq:release}\text{ is direct replace-one private for every such }
    \mathcal X,d,\text{ and oracle}.
  \]
  Privacy holds at $\sigma_{\mathrm{RO}}^*$.  This is a direct signed
  replacement result, not a two-step add/remove composition.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof idea.}
The add/remove comparison is obtained from one history-dependent but
schedule-blind Gaussian decoder, so it preserves the exact hidden
fixed-cardinality mixture.  The finite procedure verifies rational upper
bounds for that mixture.  Conversely, a constant one-dimensional oracle
recovers the formal mixture by measurable projection.  Direct replacement
cannot use the same ordinary postprocessing argument: its positive and
negative shifts are compared by a normalized signed Gaussian interpolation,
then attained by a two-record constant oracle.  Continuity, Gaussian
degradation, a feasible fallback, and a small-noise separating event turn both
exact profile feasible sets into attained upper rays.

\section{Six conventional lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[Hockey-stick calculus]\label{lem:hs}
For a Markov kernel $K$,
\[
 H_\varepsilon(PK,QK)\leq H_\varepsilon(P,Q).
\]
If $\alpha_h\geq0$ and $\sum_h\alpha_h=1$, then
\[
 H_\varepsilon\!\left(\sum_h\alpha_hP_h,\sum_h\alpha_hQ_h\right)
 \leq\sum_h\alpha_hH_\varepsilon(P_h,Q_h).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For an output test $0\leq f\leq1$, the function
$Kf(x)=\int f(y)K(x,dy)$ is an input test in $[0,1]$, which proves data
processing after taking the supremum over $f$.  For mixtures, expand the
difference of expectations as the common-weight sum of the component
differences, bound every summand by its hockey-stick divergence, and again
take the supremum.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Adaptive bounded Gaussian channel]\label{lem:channel}
If $\|v\|_2\leq1$ and $\sigma>0$, there is a channel, jointly measurable in
$v$ and its input, that sends
\[
 N(1,\sigma^2)\mapsto N(v,\sigma^2I_d),
 \qquad
 N(0,\sigma^2)\mapsto N(0,\sigma^2I_d).
\]
Consequently, if $v_t$ is a measurable function of the already decoded
history, these channels may be composed recursively into one common adaptive
decoder.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Pad $v$ to the unit vector
\[
 \widehat v=(\sqrt{1-\|v\|_2^2},v)\in\R^{d+1}.
\]
Apply a measurable Householder isometry carrying the first coordinate vector
to $\widehat v$ to $(X,Z)$, where
$Z\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ is independent, and discard coordinate zero.
Rotational invariance gives covariance $\sigma^2I_d$ in either case; the
retained mean is $v$ when $\mathbb EX=1$ and zero when $\mathbb EX=0$.
Recursively, decoded releases determine the next history and hence the next
$v_t$, so finite induction proves the asserted path-law identities.  The same
decoder is used on both privacy sides.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact hidden-schedule add/remove reduction]\label{lem:ar-upper}
Define
\[
 P_\sigma^+
 =\binom Tk^{-1}\sum_{|S|=k}N(\mathbf1_S,\sigma^2I_T),
 \qquad
 P_\sigma^0=N(0,\sigma^2I_T),
\]
and let $\Pi_{\mathrm{AR}}(\sigma)$ be the maximum of the two directed
hockey-stick divergences between them.  If
$\Pi_{\mathrm{AR}}(\sigma)\leq\delta$, then \eqref{eq:release} is
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$-private under add/remove adjacency for every admissible
oracle.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write the adjacent datasets as $C$ and $C\cup\{u\}$, and condition on the
schedules of the common users.  Their adaptive aggregate is then common to
both sides.  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:channel} at time $t$ with
$v_t=\clip(q_t(h_{<t},u))$.  The resulting decoder depends on the common-user
assignment and the decoded history, but neither on the privacy side nor on
$u$'s hidden schedule.

For a temporarily fixed $S$, induction over time shows that a scalar path with
law $N(\mathbf1_S,\sigma^2I_T)$ decodes to the conditional transcript on
$C\cup\{u\}$, whereas a centered scalar path decodes to the conditional
transcript on $C$, independently of $S$.  Therefore the \emph{single}
schedule-blind decoder maps the whole pair $(P_\sigma^+,P_\sigma^0)$ to the
two conditional transcript laws.  Data processing applies before any
componentwise privacy bound is taken.  Finally mix over the common-user
schedules, whose weights agree on both sides, and repeat with the directions
reversed.  In particular, we never average fixed-$S$ divergences; doing so
would discard hidden-schedule amplification.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite verified calibration]\label{lem:finite}
For every rational request in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, a finite rational
checker constructs a positive fallback, rechecks every generated candidate,
and returns the least accepted scale among the fallback and a finite public
grid generated from the request.  Its returned scale satisfies
$\Pi_{\mathrm{AR}}(\widehat\sigma)\leq\delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At a candidate $\sigma>0$, the exact fixed-cardinality experiment is bounded
by a two-sided scalar Gaussian profile at the conservative ratio
$\mu=k/\sigma$.  The checker accepts only finite rational witnesses whose
tail and exponential inequalities imply that this profile is at most
$\delta$.  Independently of search success, it uses
\[
 \sigma_{\mathrm{fb}}=\frac{k+1}{\delta}>0,
 \qquad
 \frac12\,\frac{k}{\sigma_{\mathrm{fb}}}
 =\frac{k\delta}{2(k+1)}\leq\delta,
\]
together with the verified Gaussian bound $\operatorname{GDP}(\mu,\varepsilon)
\leq\mu/2$.  Thus the fallback is sound.  A finite fold starts from it,
retains only rechecked candidates, and replaces the current candidate only by
a smaller accepted one.  Induction proves soundness throughout, and complete
enumeration proves minimum only within this accepted finite family.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Signed direct-replacement domination]\label{lem:signed}
Let a finite hidden family have scalar amplitudes $a_h$, vector means $m_h$,
and signed coefficients $c_h$, with
\[
 \|m_h\|_2\leq|a_h|\quad\text{for every }h.
\]
If all $c_ha_h$ are nonnegative, or all are nonpositive, then the Gaussian
stop loss of the vector family is no larger than that of the aligned scalar
family.  The comparison remains valid sequentially when positive conditional
likelihood factors from future adaptive releases multiply the $c_h$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First normalize by the positive noise scale so the reference covariance is
the identity.  Smooth $x\mapsto(x-r)_+$ and interpolate from the normalized
vector means to scalar means on one Gaussian axis.  Gaussian integration by
parts expresses the derivative as a nonnegative smoothing factor times
\[
 \sum_{h,g}W_hW_g
 \bigl(a_ha_g-\langle m_h,m_g\rangle\bigr),
\]
where $W_h$ is $c_h$ multiplied by positive likelihood factors.  Hence the
$W_ha_h$ retain one common sign.  Moreover,
\[
 |W_hW_g\langle m_h,m_g\rangle|
 \leq |W_ha_h|\,|W_ga_g|,
\]
so every compensated contribution, and therefore the derivative, is
nonnegative.  Let the smoothing vanish.  For an adaptive path, apply the
one-step result backward from the terminal stop loss; future likelihood
factors are positive and preserve alignment at each step.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lower realization and attained upper ray]\label{lem:threshold}
For both the add/remove and direct replace-one formal profiles:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item uniform privacy of all admissible oracles implies formal-profile
  feasibility;
\item on $0<k\leq T$ and $0<\delta<1$, the feasible positive scales form an
  upper ray $[\sigma^*,\infty)$ with $\sigma^*>0$ and feasibility at
  $\sigma^*$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For add/remove, take $d=1$ and the constant contribution $+1$.  Measurably
project the released scalar coordinate from the history-valued transcript.
The singleton-dataset and empty-dataset pushforwards are exactly
$P_\sigma^+$ and $P_\sigma^0$.  For replacement, use two distinct records
with constant contributions $+1$ and $-1$; projection of their singleton
transcripts gives the exact positive and negative formal laws.  Thus these
formal experiments are recovered by measurable projection, rather than being
identified with the raw history-valued transcript.

For either exact profile $\Pi$, Gaussian degradation makes $\Pi$ nonincreasing
in $\sigma$, and its finite Gaussian-mixture formula is continuous on
$(0,\infty)$.  A finite upper bound supplies a feasible scale.  Since $k>0$,
the source and reference means separate as $\sigma\downarrow0$; because
$\delta<1$, choose $\sigma_0>0$ with
$\Pi(\sigma_0)>\delta$.  Monotonicity makes every
$0<\sigma\leq\sigma_0$ infeasible.  The \emph{truncated} set
\[
 F^0=\{\sigma\in\R:\sigma_0\leq\sigma,\ \Pi(\sigma)\leq\delta\}
\]
is nonempty, closed, and bounded below, so its infimum $\sigma^*$ belongs to
$F^0$.  It is positive and feasible.  The definition of the infimum below
$\sigma^*$ and degradation above it show, for every $\sigma>0$,
\[
 \Pi(\sigma)\leq\delta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sigma^*\leq\sigma.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

\paragraph{Clause (i).}
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} gives a checked positive rational scale with
$\Pi_{\mathrm{AR}}(\widehat\sigma)\leq\delta$ and proves its stated
finite-family leastness.  Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-upper} transports that exact
formal guarantee to every adaptive clipped transcript, in both privacy
directions.  This proves the finite add/remove clause.

\paragraph{Clause (ii).}
Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-upper} gives formal feasibility $\Rightarrow$ uniform
add/remove privacy.  The constant-$+1$ projection in
Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold} gives the converse, hence
\[
 \text{uniform add/remove privacy at }\sigma
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sigma>0\ \text{and}\ \Pi_{\mathrm{AR}}(\sigma)\leq\delta.
\]
The upper-ray part of that lemma supplies the noncomputable attained least
scale $\sigma_{\mathrm{AR}}^*$.

\paragraph{Clause (iii).}
For replacement, draw the removed and inserted users' schedules independently.
After conditioning on common-user schedules and dividing each innovation by
$\sigma$, source components have scalar amplitudes $+1/\sigma$ and reference
components have amplitudes $-1/\sigma$; unselected components have amplitude
zero.  Clipping gives the corresponding normalized vector bound
$\|m_h\|_2\leq|a_h|$.

In the forward hockey-stick expression, source coefficients are positive and
reference coefficients are multiplied by $-e^\varepsilon$; hence
$c_ha_h\geq0$.  In the reverse expression the signs swap and
$c_ha_h\leq0$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:signed}, applied backward through the adaptive
path, therefore bounds both actual directions by the exact scalar pair
\[
 P_{\sigma,\mathrm{RO}}^+
 =\binom Tk^{-1}\sum_{|S|=k}N(\mathbf1_S,\sigma^2I_T),
 \qquad
 P_{\sigma,\mathrm{RO}}^-
 =\binom Tk^{-1}\sum_{|S|=k}N(-\mathbf1_S,\sigma^2I_T).
\]
Only after this signed likelihood comparison do we decode the common
normalized innovation path into the transcript.  That decoder receives the
common-user assignment, public zero initial history, oracle, and $\sigma$, but
not the privacy side or either hidden replacement schedule.  Integrating the
unused schedule and mixing over common-user assignments preserves the bound.

The two-record $+1/-1$ projection in Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold} proves the
converse.  Thus uniform direct replace-one privacy is equivalent to feasibility
of the exact two-sided profile of
$(P_{\sigma,\mathrm{RO}}^+,P_{\sigma,\mathrm{RO}}^-)$.  Its attained-upper-ray
conclusion yields the noncomputable least scale $\sigma_{\mathrm{RO}}^*$.
\qed

\section{Degenerate cases, scope, and provenance}

The finite add/remove procedure deliberately allows $k=0$ and
$\delta\geq1$.  If $k=0$, no user ever contributes, so every positive scale
has a data-independent transcript.  If $\delta\geq1$, every hockey-stick
divergence is at most $1\leq\delta$, so again every positive scale is feasible.
In either case the feasible set is $(0,\infty)$ and has infimum zero but no
least positive member.  The calibrator remains sound and returns a positive
fallback; only the claim of an attained \emph{positive} global threshold
would be false.  This is exactly why clauses (ii) and (iii) assume
$k>0$ and $\delta<1$.  The case $\delta=0$ lies outside the total-calibrator
domain: its fallback divides by $\delta$, and a nonzero Gaussian shift need
not admit any finite pure-DP scale.

The theorem concerns independent per-user uniform fixed-cardinality
schedules, unit internal clipping, measurable adaptive queries, zero initial
history, and ideal real Gaussian noise.  Measurable postprocessing of the
transcript inherits the privacy guarantee.  It does not establish conformance
of a sampler, finite-precision Gaussian generator, Python/PyTorch/Opacus
implementation, distributed execution, or experiment.  The finite rational
calibrator is add/remove only and grid-relative; the exact uniform thresholds
are noncomputable and may exceed what a particular fixed oracle needs.
