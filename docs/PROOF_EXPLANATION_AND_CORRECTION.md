# One proof explanation and correction round

Start with a real section, an author or maintainer responsible for it, and a genuine explanatory need. Draft the companion, then invite a
mathematically competent reader to choose a question independently. The fixed-epoch material
is available, but neither its selection nor production theorem adoption is a
prerequisite. Do not invent a reader question to complete the round. Without an actual
authoring need, maintain the software and wait.

The primary artifact is conventional, editable Markdown or TeX. Explain the
purpose, exact statement and conditions, main ideas and important intermediate
steps. Cite literature for mathematical ideas; a Lean declaration is not a
priority citation. Preserve uncertainty about attribution. Use examples where
they clarify the argument, without inflating the proof into a process narrative.

For a questioned step, use existing source, claim components, guide, assessments
and checking evidence as needed. A reader should reach its exact support and see
what is conventional, checked, unmapped or an attributed correspondence judgment.
Direct file reading is sufficient. No model account, service or new viewer is
required. Document the pinned environment and computational prerequisites for
any ordinary Lean check; a normal build receipt is not adversarial validation.

Keep the original passage and reader question, the response, any checked formal
obligation, and the revised passage or unresolved question. Use existing document
revision/assessment bindings when applicable; retain historical judgments rather
than making them current by changing hashes. The responsible author decides
whether to adopt the change. Record available authorized process information,
and distinguish measurements from estimates and summaries from observed events.

Preserve these distinctions during correction:

- Adding a hypothesis narrows the statement; its proof does not complete the
  original stronger claim.
- An unsuccessful application does not show that the goal is impossible.
- Conventional or unformalized mathematics is not thereby false.
- A valid alternative derivation does not verify the manuscript's proof strategy.
- Formal acceptance, prose fidelity and an understandable explanation are
  different outcomes.

End after one substantive question-and-correction round. Review the actual
passage, question, response and revision. Explain what mathematical question was
resolved and separately whether an existing Ladon operation helped. A good result
from ordinary documents and Lean remains useful without demonstrating a software
advantage. Avoid an “understood” checkbox or general understanding certification.

Only a concrete recurring manual handoff or observed mistake justifies new
software. Otherwise keep this ordinary route. Community funding, governance,
equitable model access and scholarly responsibility remain separate obligations.
